Outcomes-adjusted carbon footprint calculator for single-use vs reusable flexible ureteroscopes

The question of 'which is more environmentally sustainable?" is not a one-size-fits-all equation. Numerous factors need to be considered, such as the reusable scope lifecycle and utilization, the reprocessing footprint (including consumables required and utility draw per reprocessing cycle), and macro infrastructure (including end-of-life processes and carbon intensity of the local energy grid).
However, what is often overlooked is the impact of scope advances in single-use technology. For example, the growing availability of smaller caliber ureteroscopes may result in fewer cases of failed ureteral access in unstented patients, and therefore fewer secondary procedures and their associated footprints. 

This calculator allows users to compare the calculated carbon footprints of their single-use and reusable ureteroscope option on not only a per-case (per-scope) basis, but on a per-care episode basis (which accounts for potentially lower failed access with smaller caliber scopes).

Where users do not know inputs to their reusable ureteroscope utilization factors, or the carbon footprint of their single-use scope, we have provided default values based on currently available published Life Cycle Assessment figures (as of October 15, 2026). Since many of the numbers in this calculator are estimates, the "How sure is this?" section (Monte Carlo simulation) provides users with some more reliability. Essentially, it calculates a range of potential single-use vs disposable scope outcomes across 5000 different combinations of believable estimates for each variable and tells you, in rank-order, which variables have the greatest potential impact on the outcome.

NOTE: Carbon footprint (greenhouse gas emissions contributing to climate change) is only one of many standard environmental impact categories. Therefore, such carbon-only reporting can oversimplify and overlook the environmental trade-offs, such as fine particulate matter formation, terrestrial acidification, terrestrial/marine ecotoxicity, and mineral resource scarcity, among others.
